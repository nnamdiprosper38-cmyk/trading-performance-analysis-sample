## A. Project title

**Trading Performance Audit: A Quantitative Review of Trade Outcomes, Risk Management and Execution Discipline (Simulated Case Study)**

## B. Portfolio description

**The problem.** Traders often judge themselves by win rate or by their last few trades. A structured audit asks better questions: is the average result distinguishable from chance, how large are losses relative to wins, was risk taken as planned, where did drawdowns come from, and were the trader's own rules followed?

**The method.** I built a fictional 40-trade journal (XAUUSD, EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, BTCUSD; 2026-03-02 to 2026-04-28) with internally consistent entries, stops, position sizes, risk, costs and results. I then calculated each metric from first principles and re-checked every figure with a second, independent calculation.

**Metrics evaluated.** Win rate, profit factor, payoff ratio, expectancy with a 95% confidence interval, break-even win rate, cumulative R, drawdown (in R, USD and % of equity, kept separate), streaks, planned vs actual risk, rule-adherence flags, and segment results by setup, session, instrument and direction, always shown with sample sizes.

**Deliverables.**
- 12-page PDF report (A4) with executive summary, methodology, 5 charts, findings, a prioritised action plan, limitations and a verification appendix
- Excel workbook with live formulas, filters, frozen headers, and an OK/CHECK reconciliation against the report
- CSV export of the 40 simulated trades

**What the simulated data showed (illustrative only).** 45% win rate, +4.12R net, profit factor 1.17, maximum drawdown 3.74R (3.95% of equity). The expectancy's 95% confidence interval (-0.33R to +0.54R) includes zero, and the report says so plainly. Segment differences are labelled as too small to rank.

**Disclosure.** The data are simulated. Outcomes and rule flags were authored for the exercise, so the comparisons demonstrate technique, not market findings. The report is educational and is not investment advice.
